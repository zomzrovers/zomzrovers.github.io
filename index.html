Amex CodeStreet '26

national finalist · top 6 of 15,000+ teams · Jul – Aug 2026

A governance layer for AI agents

The question we picked was simple to state and annoying to solve: if you let AI agents spend money and call tools on your behalf, what stops one of them from going rogue?

Our answer was a fail-closed policy engine sitting between the agents and everything they touch. Every action is checked against least-privilege permissions, separation of duties, and spend and velocity limits. If the engine is unsure, the answer is no. Decisions take about 0.7 ms at p50.

The hardest bug was double spending. With many agents firing in parallel, two of them could both pass a budget check before either one was recorded. We moved state to SQLite in WAL mode with atomic cap decrements and hammered it until it held at roughly 1,700 decisions a second with zero double spends. After that came Ed25519 request signing so an agent can't pretend to be another one, replay protection and key rotation, a tamper-evident audit log, and a kill switch. We tested it against five classes of rogue-agent behaviour with 149 tests.

Getting the finalist call was one of the better moments of the year. Out of 15,000+ teams, we were one of six.
